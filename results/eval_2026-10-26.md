# Evaluation, 2026-10-26

20 faults (6 presets, 14 custom faults built by Ultra), each run once per setup. Every answer graded against the fault's hidden truth by the same grader (Super, fixed checklist: right part, right problem, right trigger). Every offered fix tried on a copy of the broken server. Costs estimated from Token Factory's list prices.

| Setup | Right cause | Partly | Wrong | Recommended fix works | Offered fixes that work | Avg time | Avg cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nano + Super + Ultra (the demo) | 15 of 20 (75%) | 5 | 0 | 20 of 20 (100%) | 26 of 30 (87%) | 29 s | $0.022 |
| Nano alone | 15 of 20 (75%) | 5 | 0 | 16 of 20 (80%) | 21 of 47 (45%) | 132 s | $0.004 |
| Super alone | 16 of 20 (80%) | 4 | 0 | 20 of 20 (100%) | 27 of 34 (79%) | 24 s | $0.011 |

## What the numbers say (written by hand after the run)

1. **Finding the cause: all three setups are close.** 15-16 of 20 right, none wrong. The right part was
   named in 18-19 of 20 runs and the right problem in 19-20. The misses are in the trigger (the "why"): 16-17 of 20.
2. **Fixing it is where they differ.** When Super or Ultra wrote the fix options, every recommended fix
   worked on the server (20 of 20). Nano alone found the cause just as often, but 4 of its recommended fixes
   didn't work (for the full database disk it moved nothing at all), only 21 of its 47 options worked,
   and 22 of its options restarted something nobody needed to restart.
3. **Time and cost.** Super alone was the fastest (24 s on average) and cost half as much as the team
   ($0.011 against $0.022 per incident). Nano alone was the cheapest ($0.004) but 4-5 times slower
   (132 s on average), because it writes about twice as much (about 9,000 output tokens per incident
   against 4,500), most of it thinking.
4. **The honest part about the 3-model team.** On these 20 faults the team was not more accurate than
   Super alone: 15 against 16 right, and 20 of 20 working recommended fixes for both. Ultra's options were
   a little more reliable (87% of its offered options worked, against 79%) with fewer unneeded restarts
   (4 against 9). With one run per fault, a difference of one fault is noise. What this shows: Super alone
   is a good and cheaper setup, and Ultra didn't measurably help on this set. Before changing the demo:
   more faults and repeated runs.
5. **Why "partly".**
   - All three setups blamed the cache crash on a Redis upgrade from 9 days earlier instead of a one-off
     bug, and none said it stayed down because the container has no restart policy. A real reasoning gap:
     an old change isn't the cause just because it's the only change on the list. The restart worked anyway.
   - 2 custom faults have logs that point to a different trigger than their hidden truth: the database
     checkpoint fault's logs say "read-only filesystem or corrupted data", and the nginx 1.28 fault's logs
     show timeouts while its truth says a 500 regression. An agent that reads those logs right still gets
     "partly". Since 26 Oct the validator rejects faults that show damaged data, like the first one.
   - One custom fault names nginx as the broken part, but its own logs (and its truth text) show the api
     failing too. The agent named the api, and the code's "right part" check said no.
6. **Limits.** Each fault ran once per setup, with the memory of past incidents switched off. The grader is
   Super, the same model as one of the setups: it grades everyone with the same checklist, but a judge model
   can prefer answers in its own style, so the fix test (no AI at all) is the more objective number.
   Costs are estimates from list prices.
